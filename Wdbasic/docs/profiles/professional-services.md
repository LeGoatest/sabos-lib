# WDBASIC Professional-Services Profile

> **Profile type:** Expertise-led service profile  
> **Core entry point:** [`../README.md`](../README.md)  
> **Token contracts:** [`../tokens/`](../tokens/)  
> **Component contract:** [`../components/component-contracts.md`](../components/component-contracts.md)

This profile applies to consultancies, agencies, accountants, legal-service providers, engineers, architects, advisors, software service firms, and other expertise-led organizations.

This profile specializes WDBASIC core. It does not replace architecture, accessibility, security, truthful-content, confidentiality, or component requirements.

## 1. Activation record

An adopting project documents:

```yaml
profile:
  name: professional-services
  audience: <client-or-buyer-profile>
  engagement_model: <project-retainer-advisory-representation-or-other>
  primary_conversion: <consultation-inquiry-assessment-or-other>
  regulated_context: <none-or-description>
  confidentiality_context: <none-or-description>
  proof_sources: <path-or-record>
  profile_exceptions: []
```

Do not infer legal privilege, fiduciary status, licensing, certification, confidentiality, guaranteed outcomes, or jurisdictional authority from the profile.

## 2. Brand position

The interface should communicate:

- Expertise.
- Judgment.
- Clear scope.
- Evidence-based outcomes.
- Responsiveness.
- Confidentiality where relevant and supportable.
- Low-friction consultation or inquiry.
- Professional accountability.

Avoid:

- Unsupported prestige language.
- Abstract corporate filler.
- Decorative complexity.
- Vague claims that conceal what the firm actually does.
- Overly casual treatment of regulated or high-stakes work.
- Generic SaaS positioning when the service depends on professional judgment.

## 3. Visual direction

- Use restrained neutral surfaces with one primary brand color and one limited action color.
- Keep content density moderate and deliberate.
- Use whitespace to clarify hierarchy, not to create empty oversized sections.
- Prefer editorial structure over generic card grids.
- Use diagrams, case-study evidence, process illustrations, and real team imagery when they improve understanding.
- Use elevation sparingly and primarily for temporary or interactive surfaces.
- Keep action styling consistent and visually distinct from ordinary editorial links.

Every visual decision remains mapped through the semantic token contracts.

## 4. Typography

Use a highly readable sans-serif body face. A restrained serif display face may support an established, editorial, legal, or advisory position when contrast, language coverage, and rendering remain reliable.

Long-form insight content should target comfortable line length and clear subheading structure.

Avoid:

- Very light body weights.
- Decorative serif faces for dense interface text.
- Oversized headings that displace service scope or consultation actions.
- Long all-capital labels.
- Unexplained technical or legal jargon.

Typography follows [`../tokens/typography.md`](../tokens/typography.md).

## 5. Trust architecture

Make it easy to verify:

- Services and engagement types.
- Industries or client profiles served.
- Team qualifications that are factually supported.
- Representative outcomes or case studies.
- Process, timeline, and expected client participation.
- Contact and consultation pathways.
- Jurisdictional, licensing, confidentiality, or independence limitations where relevant.
- Who is responsible for the work.
- Whether the initial inquiry creates any formal relationship.

Do not display client logos, awards, certifications, success rates, testimonials, or outcome metrics without a defined source and permission.

## 6. Evidence and proof

Preferred proof includes:

- Case studies describing the initial problem, work, and result.
- Named credentials with issuer and current status.
- Published work or maintained resources.
- Representative deliverables with permission.
- Outcome metrics with timeframe, sample, and source.
- Testimonials with sufficient context and consent.
- Team biographies tied to actual responsibilities.

Avoid anonymous prestige signals and decontextualized metrics.

Where confidentiality prevents detailed proof, explain the type of work and method without inventing specifics.

## 7. Recommended homepage sequence

1. Clear expertise and outcome statement.
2. Primary consultation or inquiry action.
3. Service or problem-area navigation.
4. Evidence of expertise.
5. Case studies or representative outcomes.
6. Engagement process.
7. Team or responsible-practitioner context.
8. Insight or resource preview where maintained.
9. FAQ, suitability, or qualification criteria.
10. Final consultation action.

The page should answer:

- What problem does the firm solve?
- For whom?
- What type of engagement is offered?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- What happens after an inquiry?
- What does not happen merely because the visitor submitted a form?

## 8. Service and capability pages

Each service page should define:

- Problem or decision addressed.
- Scope of work.
- Deliverables.
- Engagement model.
- Client responsibilities.
- Relevant limitations.
- Evidence or representative outcomes.
- Related expertise or resources.
- Clear consultation or inquiry action.

Distinguish advice, implementation, representation, assessment, training, and ongoing support.

Avoid creating thin pages that differ only by industry name or keyword.

## 9. Recommended components

- Editorial hero.
- Service or capability navigation.
- Expertise and credential block.
- Case-study summary.
- Outcome metric with source.
- Team profile.
- Engagement-process steps.
- Insight or article card.
- Qualification or eligibility panel.
- Consultation form.
- Disclosure and legal-context block.
- Related-services navigation.
- Download or resource panel with accurate file information.
- Loading, empty, validation, error, and success states.

All components follow [`../components/component-contracts.md`](../components/component-contracts.md).

## 10. Consultation and inquiry forms

Forms should:

- Ask only for information needed to route or evaluate the inquiry.
- Explain expected next steps.
- Clarify whether submission creates a client, advisory, legal, fiduciary, or confidential relationship.
- Avoid requesting sensitive or privileged information before a secure relationship exists.
- Provide secure alternatives when sensitive information is required.
- Preserve input after recoverable validation errors.
- Distinguish an inquiry from a confirmed appointment or engagement.
- Avoid promising response times not supported by operations.

A success state confirms receipt and the next procedural step without implying acceptance of the engagement.

## 11. Regulated and high-stakes contexts

When the service is regulated, licensed, jurisdictional, or high-stakes, document:

- Applicable jurisdiction or market.
- Responsible professional or entity.
- Required disclosures.
- Limits of website content.
- Whether content is general information rather than individualized advice.
- Secure communication requirements.
- Record-retention or consent requirements where applicable.

Do not use a generic disclaimer to compensate for misleading service claims or unclear scope.

## 12. Insights and resources

Publish resources only when the organization can maintain them.

Resource pages should provide:

- Clear author or responsible organization.
- Publication and material-update dates.
- Topic and audience.
- Internal links to relevant services.
- Appropriate references or source context.
- Review or expiration workflow for time-sensitive content.

Do not create thin AI-generated insight pages solely for search volume.

## 13. Team presentation

Team profiles should identify:

- Name and role.
- Actual responsibility.
- Relevant, verified qualifications.
- Jurisdiction or license scope where applicable.
- Appropriate contact or inquiry path.

Avoid decorative team grids with invented biographies, vague titles, or unsupported credentials.

## 14. Content rules

Use specific language about:

- Problems.
- Deliverables.
- Decisions.
- Responsibilities.
- Outcomes.
- Limitations.
- Next steps.

Avoid:

- “Trusted advisor,” “leading,” “premier,” or “world-class” without evidence.
- Guarantees of success.
- Client logos without permission.
- Metrics without source and timeframe.
- Artificial urgency.
- Claims that an inquiry is confidential or privileged unless operationally and legally supportable.

## 15. Profile compliance checklist

- Is the audience and engagement model documented?
- Is the primary consultation or inquiry action clear?
- Are services, deliverables, and limitations specific?
- Is evidence contextual and attributable?
- Are credentials current and verified?
- Does the site explain what happens after an inquiry?
- Are regulated, jurisdictional, and confidentiality limits clear where relevant?
- Are sensitive-information requests minimized and secured?
- Is editorial content maintained and dated appropriately?
- Are team responsibilities and qualifications factual?
- Are accessibility, architecture, and component contracts still satisfied?
