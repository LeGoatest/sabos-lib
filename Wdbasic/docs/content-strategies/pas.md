# PAS Strategy — When Applicable

> **Status:** Practitioner content strategy; applicability controlled

Problem–Agitate–Solution (PAS) is useful when a visitor is meaningfully problem-aware and relevant consequences help them evaluate a credible response.

## Applicability

Record PAS as:

```text
pas_applicability: yes | partial | no
```

Use `yes` when problem recognition and consequences are central to the decision. Use `partial` when only some elements are useful. Use `no` when an answer, comparison, offer, navigation path, tool, or evidence should lead instead.

## Semantic progression

When PAS applies, a page may distribute the jobs across components:

```text
Problem recognition
→ relevant consequence
→ solution
→ validation / proof
→ objection resolution
→ action
```

This is not a mandatory visual template and does not require the problem to appear before the solution in every context.

## Ethical agitation

Agitation means explaining relevant consequence, inconvenience, risk, cost, delay, or missed outcome. Claims must remain proportional, supportable, and directly connected to the problem.

Do not manufacture fear, catastrophize, create fake urgency, or repeatedly paraphrase the same pain.

## Anti-repetition rule

A later component should not merely reword an earlier semantic job unless it adds evidence, specificity, context, a new user question, a decision criterion, an example, or material buyer-stage value.

## Historical scoring

Past WDBASIC research explored weighted PAS formulas including `P(7)+A(5)+S(8)` and a generic weighted form. Those are preserved as historical heuristics under the measurable-evidence research record and are not canonical strategy requirements.
