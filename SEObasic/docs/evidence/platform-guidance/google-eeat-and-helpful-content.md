# Google E-E-A-T and Helpful, People-First Content

> **Status:** Current Google platform-guidance record  
> **Source class:** Platform documentation / guidance  
> **Publisher:** Google Search Central  
> **Scope:** Google Search content-quality self-assessment, E-E-A-T, quality raters, and people-first content  
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-08-11  
> **Authority:** Google-specific guidance; not a universal web standard or SEObasic ranking-factor contract

## Google position

Google describes E-E-A-T as:

- **Experience**
- **Expertise**
- **Authoritativeness**
- **Trustworthiness**

Current Google Search Central guidance says its automated systems use many factors and can identify a mix of factors associated with E-E-A-T.

Important boundaries:

- **E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor.**
- Google states that **trust is the most important** of the four aspects.
- Content does not necessarily need to demonstrate all four aspects equally.
- Google gives stronger consideration to strong E-E-A-T alignment for some YMYL topics.
- Search Quality Raters are trained to assess E-E-A-T, but rater judgments do not directly determine the ranking of an individual page or site.

SEObasic MUST NOT convert E-E-A-T into a fabricated score, guaranteed ranking checklist, or claim that adding specific page elements will cause rankings to improve.

## Experience

Experience concerns relevant first-hand or life experience.

Depending on the subject, useful evidence can include:

- original project documentation;
- real photographs or video;
- before-and-after evidence;
- first-hand observations;
- lessons from actual work;
- practical constraints encountered in real projects;
- direct use of a product, service, place, process, or method.

Do not fabricate first-hand experience or present stock, generated, illustrative, or hypothetical material as completed real-world work.

## Expertise

Expertise concerns relevant knowledge or skill.

It can be supported by:

- accurate explanations;
- meaningful subject depth;
- correct terminology used naturally;
- clear process, limitations, tradeoffs, and prerequisites;
- relevant credentials when real and applicable;
- genuine subject-matter review when it actually occurred;
- reliable sourcing where outside support is needed.

Expertise is contextual. Different topics require different forms and levels of knowledge.

## Authoritativeness

Authoritativeness concerns whether the creator, publisher, or source is recognized as relevant to the subject.

Potential supporting evidence can include:

- relevant reputation;
- independent references or citations;
- genuine reviews;
- relevant mentions or links from credible sources;
- recognized professional participation;
- a consistent body of useful first-party work.

Self-declared authority is not independent evidence. Claims such as “industry leader,” “top expert,” or similar statements remain subject to the [`Truth and Evidence Contract`](../contracts/truth-and-evidence.md).

## Trustworthiness

Google states that trust is the most important E-E-A-T aspect.

Trust can be supported by:

- accurate publisher or business identity;
- truthful service, location, credential, and outcome claims;
- clear authorship where readers would expect it;
- useful author or organization background;
- sourcing for material factual claims;
- transparent methods or process descriptions;
- accurate review and testimonial representation;
- clear distinction between real evidence and examples;
- correction of factual errors;
- appropriate disclosure of material automation or AI involvement where readers would reasonably expect to know how content was created.

Trust cannot be manufactured by adding unsupported badges, biographies, testimonials, schema properties, or “expert-reviewed” labels.

## Who, How, and Why

Google recommends evaluating content in terms of **Who, How, and Why**.

### Who created it?

Make the creator or publisher clear where readers would reasonably expect that information. Use bylines and creator background when appropriate, but do not treat a byline as a magic ranking field.

### How was it created?

Explain method, testing, observation, project work, research, review, or automation when that process materially affects a reader's ability to judge the content.

### Why was it created?

The primary reason should be to help people. Search optimization can support useful content; it should not replace useful content.

## SEObasic application

E-E-A-T should influence the quality of content and evidence rather than become a decorative page section.

A useful pattern is:

```text
real customer problem
→ accurate explanation
→ credible solution/process
→ first-party or sourced evidence
→ clear creator/business provenance
→ useful next action
```

This connects naturally to [`../content/customer-pain-and-solution-framing.md`](../content/customer-pain-and-solution-framing.md).

Examples:

- Case studies can demonstrate experience through real conditions, work performed, project evidence, and outcomes.
- Service pages can demonstrate expertise by explaining process, scope, limitations, preparation, and common project conditions.
- About and author pages can make relevant people, responsibilities, qualifications, and business history easier to verify.
- Independent reviews, citations, references, and mentions can provide evidence beyond the publisher's own claims.

None of these elements independently guarantees ranking improvement.

## Anti-patterns

SEObasic rejects:

- invented E-E-A-T scores presented as Google metrics;
- fake authors, reviewers, credentials, awards, affiliations, or experience;
- unsupported “expert reviewed” labels;
- fake or misleading reviews and testimonials;
- illustrative imagery represented as actual project evidence;
- copied material lightly rewritten to simulate depth;
- repetitive “Experience,” “Expertise,” “Authority,” and “Trust” blocks added mechanically to pages;
- assuming structured data creates expertise or authority unsupported by visible content and real evidence;
- treating backlinks, reviews, schema, author pages, or credentials as interchangeable with E-E-A-T itself;
- manufacturing length through repetitive paraphrasing.

## Relationship to SEObasic contracts

Google's E-E-A-T material is external platform guidance. SEObasic's [`Truth and Evidence Contract`](../contracts/truth-and-evidence.md) is separately binding.

```text
Google platform guidance
        ↓ informs
SEObasic application guidance
        ↓ constrained by
Truth and Evidence Contract
```

Google guidance does not silently redefine SEObasic contracts.

## Review questions

```text
Does the content show real first-hand experience when experience matters?
Does it demonstrate appropriate expertise for the subject?
Can readers understand who is responsible for it?
Can important claims be verified?
Is methodology clear when it affects trust?
Is independent evidence represented accurately?
Is the content primarily useful to people rather than manufactured for rankings?
Have we avoided turning E-E-A-T into a fake score or ranking formula?
```
