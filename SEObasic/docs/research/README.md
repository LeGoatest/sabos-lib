# SEObasic Research

> **Scope:** Scholarly research, empirical studies, experiments, measurement methodology, evidence synthesis, and limitations relevant to search, answer/generative discovery, local visibility, content, paid media, social media, websites, entities, and YouTube.

Research in SEObasic exists to test, challenge, refine, or support documented positions. It does not automatically become a contract.

- [`answer-generative-discovery.md`](answer-generative-discovery.md) — AEO/GEO, generative search, source selection, citation/attribution, source influence, referral measurement, benchmarks, optimization risks, peer-reviewed/preprint classification, and research-discovery resources including Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, ACL Anthology, ACM/KDD, DBLP, Crossref and OpenAlex.

## Evidence model

Research records SHOULD preserve:

- research question;
- source/citation;
- publication status (peer-reviewed, preprint, benchmark/dataset, etc.);
- study or dataset scope;
- methodology;
- relevant finding;
- limitations/confounders;
- date and platform context;
- relationship to existing SEObasic positions or contracts.

## Research-discovery rule

Indexes such as Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, DBLP, Crossref, OpenAlex and citation databases are useful for finding and tracing work. They do not replace reading the primary paper, proceedings record, DOI source, dataset or author manuscript.

An arXiv manuscript is a primary research source but arXiv presence alone does not establish peer review.

## Rule

A correlation study does not prove causation. A vendor experiment does not automatically generalize across industries, geographies, or platforms. A practitioner result remains valuable evidence but must be labeled as such. A controlled GEO/RAG benchmark does not automatically expose a production search platform's ranking factors.
